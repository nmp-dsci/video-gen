MiniMax H3 from zero: one harness, four techniques, your first clip this week

You asked what MiniMax H3 is, whether people run it in ComfyUI, whether you need to rent a GPU, and how to make videos that remember a character over time. This page answers those from the transcripts in transcript-rag-agent (three H3 videos plus nine on neighbouring tools), the official model card, the ComfyUI docs, and fal and Modal docs. It then lays out the project: a small, reproducible generation harness, a bake-off of four H3 techniques on one fixed brief, and a character LoRA so the same character survives across many clips. The first test costs nothing and can run today.

Your four questions, answered first

The short version

Is it ComfyUI?

Mostly, yes. MiniMax H3 has official ComfyUI templates (text-to-video, image-to-video, reference-to-video, multiframe reference, ControlNet) from ComfyUI 0.30 on, and every creator in the transcripts drives it either through ComfyUI or through a hand-rolled Gradio notebook on Kaggle. ComfyUI is the runtime, though, not the harness: you drive it from code through its API-format workflow JSON, so every run is a record, not a click.

Do I need to rent a GPU?

Yes. H3 is a 33-billion-parameter model; your Mac (M1 Pro, 32 GB, no CUDA) cannot run it. You have three rentals to hand: Kaggle's free dual T4 (the notebook already exists), Modal (your CLI is logged in; L40S $1.95/h, H100 $3.95/h, $30 free a month), and fal (hosted H3 endpoints and H3 LoRA trainers, pay per clip or per training step). The Mac stays for the harness code, the judging and the review pages.

How are people using MiniMax?

Three ways. (1) Hosted: the H3 Max API on fal or MiniMax's platform, no setup, commercial rights included. (2) Open weights in ComfyUI: pruned INT8 builds plus 4- or 8-step turbo LoRAs so a 16–24 GB card can play. (3) Community layers on top: fine-tuned checkpoints (Singularity), adapters that steer motion (SolarRenderer turns a skeleton, a rough 3D scene or a drawing into a finished clip that moves the same way; Fun ControlNet Union does it from pose or depth video; see 2.4) and custom LoRAs trained on their own clips.

How do the videos remember?

Three layers, cheapest first. Per shot: reference-to-video takes up to 9 images, 3 videos and 3 audio clips of your character. Per project: a LoRA trained on 50–200 clips of that character, loaded into every generation. Across shots: multi-shot prompts inside one 15 s clip, then a chaining tool such as the H3 Extender to go past 15 s. H3 has no trained memory model like Wan 2.2's StoryMem, so the harness rebuilds StoryMem's keyframe memory out of H3's own reference slots. Section 4 has the diagram.

1 · What MiniMax H3 is

A video-and-audio model, released as weights, with a hosted top tier kept back

From the model card (MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3) and the ComfyUI tutorial, cross-checked against the three transcripts. Nothing here is from memory: the model is newer than my training data.

MiniMax (the Hailuo video family). Open weights since the H3 release; the licence is theirs, not Apache.
backbone
H3-Omni-Transformer, "33B-parameter dense, single-stream Transformer"; text from Qwen3-VL-32B hidden states (layer 50); visual VAE 16× spatial, 4× temporal; audio VAE at 32 kHz.
output
4–15 s, 24 fps, short edge 768 px (1344×768 at 16:9; sides in multiples of 32), stereo audio with dialogue, SFX and music generated in the same pass.

frames
Length is a frame count on a 17k+5 grid: 124 frames ≈ 5.2 s (default), 192 ≈ 8 s, 345 ≈ 14.4 s.
steps
Base: 20 steps, "about 25 for motion quality". Turbo: 8 steps with the official fl2v LoRA, 4 with the official ref2v LoRA. cfg 1, no negative prompt.
ComfyUI files
Comfy-Org/MiniMax-H3: minimax_h3_fl2va_pruned_int8_convrot and …ref2va_pruned_int8_convrot (diffusion_models), qwen3vl_32b_minimax_h3_nvfp4_awq (text_encoders), video VAE int8 + audio VAE fp32 (vae), turbo LoRAs fl2v_turbo_8step_v1.0 and ref2v_turbo_4step_v0.1 (loras), fun_controlnet_union_2.0 (model_patches). 2 · The techniques around H3

Seven levers, each with what it buys you, what it costs, and the evidence

"Super useful" in the transcripts means four things: faster, more faithful to a character, more controllable, and cleaner at the end. The levers below map onto those. The bake-off in section 8 tests the first four head to head; the rest are milestones.

2.1 · Turbo and distillation LoRAs: speed, with a known tax

H3 at 20–25 steps is slow on rented hardware. Four-step distillations cut that by 5–6× and, on free T4s, make it usable at all. The AIQuest comparison ran three of them plus the official 8-step model on the same prompts (rEjasJZq1t8). The pattern: every distillation is sharper or smoother or faster, and each gives something back.

2.2 · Fine-tuned checkpoints: Singularity, and why "newer" is a variable, not an upgrade

The video you sent (1xjB1c00hM4, "The Ai Blueprint") is a V1.3-vs-V2 test of WarmBloodAban/Minimax-h3_Singularity, a community fusion of H3's ref, fl and b25-49 checkpoints, "deep fine-tuned and then pruned" to INT8 (ComfyUI Wiki). It runs in the same ComfyUI workflow as base H3 and supports text-, image- and reference-to-video. V2 (21 Oct 2026) adds "expanded action coverage, improved prompt adherence, audio fixes, and enhanced visual styles" and fixes the audio-reference path that V1 did not condition on.

How he ran it
  • 9 s clip at ~1.5 MP, portrait widescreen; "a LoRA and a reference image to help maintain the character's identity" (2:34)
  • 10 steps, Euler sampler, beta scheduler; "you can experiment with six steps" (2:58); patch attention on for speed (3:11)
  • DLSS-R at 1.5× with quality −1: "results weren't consistently better" (3:39)
  • Two-character test: identity references for "girl one and girl two", 8 s, both walk in and say "Welcome" (5:40). Both identities held in both versions.
What he found
  • "At first glance, both versions look fairly similar" (4:55); he still prefers V1.3's lighting.
  • "Version two's audio sounded better overall" (7:56), the one clear win.
  • "I wouldn't recommend switching models purely because the newer version is available" (7:38).

Lesson for the harness: the checkpoint is a logged variable with a seed-matched A/B, never a silent default. The same test that caught "V2 ≈ V1.3" is the test that will catch a real regression later.

2.3 · Reference-to-video: identity without training

H3's ref2va mode takes your character as pictures, video and audio and is told, in the prompt, which reference is which. This is the cheapest consistency you can buy: zero training, one reference sheet reused for every shot.

  • Up to 9 images, 3 videos, 3 audio clips. Tag each <Picture N>, <Video N>, <Audio N>; ref_image_0 is <Picture 1>. One image can hold several panels (a front/side/expression sheet), and a shot can point at "the full front view of <Picture 2>".
  • Audio references carry timbre, not words: write the delivery in the prompt, one audio reference per speaker when assignment must be exact. Mark each audio as fully_copy, partially_copy, reference or weak_reference.
  • Reference video is read as 24 fps with no metadata: convert first. ref_image_size: max keeps a 2048 px short edge but is "much slower".
  • When references must hold, run 20–25 steps without the 4-step LoRA, and keep style LoRAs near 0.5.

2.4 · Structure control: Fun ControlNet Union and SolarRenderer

Two ways to dictate motion rather than describe it. Fun ControlNet Union 2.0 (ComfyUI 0.38+) takes Canny, Depth, HED, MLSD or Pose control video and masked inpainting. SolarRenderer (uYLdk6Hkq8k) goes further: "any structure in, any world out". You give it the motion as a rough video (a Mixamo skeleton, a 3D body mesh, moving boxes, an animated child's drawing), a prompt and, optionally, a starting frame (0:08). It renders a finished H3 clip that follows "the choreography from the reference instead of making up a new dance that vaguely fits the prompt" (1:44). Inside, it is two adapters on the frozen 33B backbone. One lines each structure frame up with the output frame it describes, "like putting the right animation drawing under the right frame on a light table" (8:59); the other is a 4-step speed adapter.

  • 16 structure types in its evaluation, 11 unseen in training: skeletons, meshes, depth, normal maps, voxels, moving boxes, children's drawings (1:15).
  • 4-step version samples ~12.3× faster than its 50-step control model; Elo 1,510 vs 1,410 (Lin Real Omni) and 1,189 (ControlNet Union), Gemini as judge plus a blind human check (0:34–1:05).
  • Real-time: gameplay re-rendered as a stream about 1.1 s behind the input (7:21).
  • "The documented low-memory ComfyUI workflow targets a 16 GB GPU using 8-bit models and offloading" (9:54). Code Apache-2.0; weights under the H3 community licence.
  • It controls motion and layout, not identity: "a box by itself doesn't tell you what object it should become" (2:45). Who the character is still comes from the prompt, a starting frame or a LoRA.
  • "A rough reference can contribute to a rough result" (4:15): the upside-down pose in the four-person dance came out less smooth.
  • Use for you: lock choreography with a Mixamo skeleton once, then re-skin the same move as astronaut, armour or ink (5:25), the "character concept" test he calls out. Here that is bake-off arm S, probably at M4: the motion from a skeleton, Mara from a starting frame. Whether a character LoRA stacks with its adapters is untested.

2.5 · A custom LoRA: the character that survives the whole project

What a LoRA is. A small file of learned adjustments that loads on top of the frozen 33B model for one run, at a strength (scale) you choose. Scale 0 switches it off, and the base model never changes. Two kinds appear in this plan, and only one of them is about your character. Pictures of the character are a third thing, and they need no LoRA at all:

Clips, not stills. fal's t2v, i2v and ref2va trainers all reject a zip of images, or a zip that mixes images and video. Pictures go in only through the ref2va trainer, as up to four reference files beside each clip (clip01.ref_1.png to ref_4); a clip without them is trained against one of its own frames. Inline Studio's trainer (v1.2.62 on) is the one that learns from stills: it "trains on still images, and the adapter applies to video", so it learns the look but not motion or voice, and it fits a 16 GB card from v1.2.63. For a new character, then: draw the sheet, let ref2va turn it into clips, and train on the clips (the last paragraph of this card).

H3 LoRAs are trainable today. fal runs four trainers (minimax/h3/t2v/trainer, i2v, flf2v, ref2va); Ostris AI-Toolkit added a minimax_h3 extension on 3 Aug 2026 (RunComfy hosts it); Fizgig lists H3 too. One dated blog says H3 cannot be LoRA-trained; the trainer docs and fal's published 16-run campaign say otherwise. Where the clips come from when the character is new: generate them. Design the character once as a reference sheet with an image model, render 60–100 short clips with the winning bake-off arm and ref2va, keep the ones where the identity held, caption them, train. The LoRA then carries the identity without needing the sheet in every prompt, and the sheet stays as a belt-and-braces reference.

2.6 · The prompt format: the free lever with the biggest swing

H3 was trained on structured prompts, and the ComfyUI prompt guide is specific. Singularity's author: "the way you structure your prompt can make a noticeable difference" (1:40). The rules that matter:

  • Three fields: integrated_multimodal_description (shots, camera, dialogue), overall_soundscape (1–4 sentences of ambience and SFX, N/A only if silent), non_diegetic_music (score only, 1–3 sentences or N/A).
  • Shots are numbered; [Shot 1] starts at 0.00 s; later shots open with one cut time: [Shot 2] At 00:03.500, the camera cuts to…. No ranges, times increase, cuts phrased as actions.
  • Speech: speaker ids (S1), (S2) outside the tag, words inside <d>[English] …</d> with punctuation, no ellipses, in the shot where they are spoken. Off-screen voice: say "in an off-screen voiceover" and that lips stay closed.
  • No negative prompts: cfg is 1, so a ban list adds the concepts it names. Describe what should be there.
  • 1344×768 native at 16:9; I2V templates open at 640×640 and need the aspect ratio set; a first frame is stretched to the canvas, a last frame centre-cropped.

A complete brief in the format (written to the guide's spec for the first test; an original character, no real likeness):

integrated_multimodal_description: A neon-lit night market in heavy rain. [Shot 1] A woman in her thirties with short silver hair and a yellow rain jacket (Mara) stands under a stall awning shaking water from her sleeve; handheld medium shot, shallow depth of field, cyan and magenta neon reflected on wet stone. (S1) She looks just past the camera and says, half-smiling, <d>[English] You said it never rains here.</d> [Shot 2] At 00:04.500, the camera cuts to a low wide shot across the puddled street as she steps out into the rain and walks toward a parked scooter, steam rising from food stalls on both sides.
overall_soundscape: Steady rain on canvas awnings, distant sizzling woks, a scooter idling, muffled market chatter.
non_diegetic_music: N/A

8 s → 192 frames (17×11+5). It has one identity, one line, one hard cut and one wide shot: the four things the transcripts show distillations fail on, so the bake-off has something to measure.

2.7 · Post: upscale last, and only what survived

  • FlashVSR+ (e1sJ78nHLDM): 1.3B one-step video super-resolution; 480p→1080p for a 5 s clip in 2 min 1 s on Kaggle's two T4s; 0.98 fps on a 4090 vs SeedVSR 0.18; "large motion" modes cost 2–4×; 4K "extremely slow" on free GPUs. It "will not magically erase broken structural artifacts that were baked in by your original AI video generator" (3:45).
  • Hosted alternatives on fal: SeedVR2 (temporally consistent) and Topaz.
  • Resolution discipline from the H3 tests: iterate at 480p, render finals at 540p or 720p, and frame faces close; a wide shot at 480p is where faces melt.

4 · Memory

How a character survives from clip to clip, in three layers

What the other tools do about it

  • StoryMem (GM70MDyl94E; github.com/Kevin-thu/StoryMem): writes a story shot by shot on Wan 2.2 A14B. After each shot it extracts keyframes automatically into a memory of up to 10 shots, and a 0.7B memory-to-video LoRA conditions the next shot on them; two variants also link neighbouring shots through the first frame or the first 5 motion frames when there is no cut, and your reference images can seed the memory. The video reports 28.7% better consistency than the baseline and 9.4% over HoloCine; ~16 GB VRAM with an FP8/FP4 backbone. Licence: S-Lab License 1.0, non-commercial only. Not related to PDMD (2.1), which is a speed distillation for H3. Nothing trained like it exists for H3; the table below shows what does.
  • Seedance 2.5 (rIM4N6CPyho, bNDcnDoqYoo): hosted only (BytePlus Lumina / fal / Higgsfield); 30 s single renders, "multi-round extensions" to ~2 min, up to 50 references cited in the prompt with @. It is the quality reference in every AIQuest comparison ("Seedance 2.5, which nailed this test"). A fair ceiling arm, not a thing you can own.
  • LTX 2.5 (QMlrXTFFbLU, Dfrdf_YhKGM): open weights (gated), native multi-shot where "lighting, environment, and even the voice hold up across cuts"; 22B distill runs on Kaggle at ~7 GB VRAM but 457 s per 5 s clip at 480p, with a recurring hallucinated extra person. A credible second open model if H3's licence is a problem for you.

What the harness does about it

  • A character bible is data, committed: characters/mara/sheet.png (front, three-quarter, profile, two expressions), voice.wav, bible.yaml (the fixed descriptive sentence used verbatim in every prompt), and later lora/mara-v1.safetensors with its training record.
  • Every job that names a character gets the sheet and the sentence injected by the prompt builder, so consistency is not a thing you remember to do.
  • Identity is a measured score (section 8), tracked per character across runs in MLflow, so you can see drift as a line, not a feeling.

Past 15 seconds on H3: what exists (checked 31 Oct 2026)

No trained memory model like StoryMem exists for H3. H3 needs one less: its reference mode already takes up to 9 images, 3 videos and 3 audio clips per generation, which is the input StoryMem had to train Wan 2.2 to accept. Community ComfyUI packs use that to chain clips. None of them picks keyframes from earlier shots by itself: each carries the previous clip plus the references you choose by hand.

The memory loop the harness adds: StoryMem's idea, rebuilt with H3 references

Seed the memory. Shot 1 runs with the character sheet in fixed reference slots, the way the Extender's global references work, plus the character LoRA once M3 has produced one.shot 1
Harvest after every shot. Keep one or two frames where the character is clearly visible, chosen by the identity score from section 8, and store them under the shot id.automatic, no hand-picking
Fill the next shot's slots. Sheet first, then the newest memory frames. When all 9 image slots are full, the oldest memory frame drops out. StoryMem remembers up to 10 shots; H3's ceiling is 9 images, sheet included.rolling window
Continuation or cut. If the next shot continues the scene, pass the last frames as motion context through the Extender. On a hard cut, skip that and rely on the memory frames alone.per shot, from the brief
Record the memory. The memory state goes into the job record with the hash of every reference, so any shot can be re-rendered with exactly the memory it had (rule 1).reproducible

How M4 tests it. One six-shot story, same seeds, three ways: the Extender with the sheet only; the Extender plus the memory loop; and the memory loop plus the M3 LoRA. The identity score per shot decides which one stays. StoryMem itself stays out of the bake-off: it would add Wan 2.2 as a second model, and its licence is non-commercial. It comes in as a research control only if all three H3 runs drift.

5 · The harness

One job record, three providers, everything cached and logged

This is the src/video_gen/ that AGENTS.md sketches, made concrete. It keeps rules 1–4: every clip reproducible from its record, every paid call cached, cost known before it is spent, one module for every generation call.

The job record (what reproducibility means here)

identity
brief_id, shot_id, arm, character
model
checkpoint name + sha256, text encoder, VAEs, each LoRA with strength, patches
prompt
the three H3 fields verbatim, plus the bible sentence version
sampling
seed, width×height, frames, fps, steps, sampler, scheduler, cfg

sha256 of every image/audio/video reference and its tag

name, GPU type, wall-clock, measured cost (or quoted price), job/request id

The cache key is the hash of identity + model + prompt + sampling + references. Same key, no second bill. vg bakeoff --dry-run prints the job count and the estimate before anything is sent; a per-run cap refuses to start past it.

MLflow

Experiment video-gen/bakeoff (later video-gen/lora), tags project, git_sha, env=local, billing=<provider>. Clips are logged by path and key, not as artifacts (they are large); the record JSON and the judge CSV are artifacts. Onboarding into registry/projects.yaml with a zero-cost vg smoke happens before the first tracked run, as AGENTS.md says.

Cost before anything runs

  • On Kaggle's two T4s a 5 s 480p 4-step clip took 182 s with the transformer split across cards; a single L40S should be several times faster. Budget 1–3 min per 480p turbo clip and 3–6 min per 720p 20-step clip on L40S at $1.95/h.
  • 48 open clips, mixed: roughly 2–4 GPU hours ≈ $4–8, plus cold starts and the 720p finals. Call it $5–15. Inside Modal's $30 monthly credit.
  • 12 hosted clips at fal's per-clip rate (on the model page; not fetched here).
  • vg bakeoff --dry-run prints the exact count and estimate; the cap is set at $25 for M2.

Reading the result

Expect what the transcripts found: B sharper and more faithful than A, C close to A visually with better audio, D the only one that holds identity in the wide shot, R ahead on everything and not yours. The decision the numbers make is which open arm becomes the default for M3's dataset generation.

9 · Risks and open questions

What could make this plan wrong

Licence geography

If you are in an excluded territory, open-weight generation and reportedly its outputs are outside the grant until MiniMax approves the form. The plan survives (fal, Comfy Cloud, or LTX 2.5 as the open model), but M1's free path moves to a paid one. decide first

VRAM on the rented card

Measured peaks (~32 GB on a 5090, 46 GB on a 4090 with raw weights for long clips) say L40S (48 GB) is the floor and 15 s at 768p may need an A100-80. The harness picks the GPU per job; the first Modal run is a 5 s clip to measure.

Distillation tax on identity

Every 4-step LoRA is "weaker on reference and audio adherence". Arm D runs at 20–25 steps for that reason; M3's dataset should be generated the same way, or the LoRA learns the distillation's softness.

Dataset quality for the LoRA

fal kept 176 of 253 clips and still got "floaty" output from slow motion. Hand review and a strict 24.000 fps are the two things that decide M3, not the rank.

Model churn

Singularity V2 landed eight days before this page; Comfy templates moved from 0.30 to 0.38 in weeks. Every arm pins filenames and hashes; a new checkpoint is a new arm, never an in-place swap.

Kaggle quota and tooling drift

The notebook is one creator's; "I recently combed through and patched several older tools that had broken." It is M1's tool, not M2's: the harness never depends on it.

Likeness and rights

Original characters only (rule 6). Reference audio for a voice must be yours or licensed. Generated media stays out of git except curated samples.

Hosted H3 Max ≠ open H3

The hosted tier has Context-IR pre-processing and 2K regeneration; the open weights do not. Arm R is a ceiling, and the gap it shows is partly those two components, not only the weights.

Long-form tools are one person's code

The Extender's author calls it the "final feature release", and its audio joins are unfinished; the other chaining packs changed weekly in October. The harness pins each pack to a commit and owns the memory loop itself, so a pack can be swapped without losing the method. StoryMem's S-Lab licence is non-commercial, so it can only ever be a research control.
